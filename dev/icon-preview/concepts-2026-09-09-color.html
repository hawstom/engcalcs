Round 2 settled the geometry; this sheet answers the color question and the maskable one. Every path is round 2's, unchanged: the only new geometry is a closed body path per tower (round 2's own numbers, joined so there is something to fill) and the W in the one LW row, which no scan of the sketch can supply and which is marked OURS where it appears. The pictures are REAL PNGs, rendered through headless Chromium at 16 / 32 / 48 / 192 / 512 px on a white and on a dark page ground, shown at 1:1 (only the 512 is scaled down, to 256, so a row fits on a screen). The ΔL table under each verdict is the measurement described below.

The catwalk's middle, measured

Tom predicted the color tweak is lossy in the raster respect because the catwalk loses its middle. The catwalk is a bar crossing the tank, so its middle exists exactly when the pixels ON the bar differ from the body pixels just above and below. Each PNG is decoded and sampled on a column INSIDE the tank (the center for the tall towers; x = 17 for WT-WIDE, whose crown carries the letter): ΔL is the luminance difference (0-255) between the strongest row within half a stroke of the bar and the mean of the body rows 2 units either side. Green is ΔL ≥ 26, a tenth of the range, which is about where a difference stops being findable on a screen at arm's length. Red is a bar with no middle.

What the rasters said

  • The catwalk answer, and it is not the one he expected. On the solid-blue rows the middle is gone at EVERY size, 512 included, not just in the raster: a bar the same color as the body it crosses has nothing to be seen against. It is a color decision rather than a resolution one, so a bigger icon does not fix it and only a second color does (the knockout row).
  • On every silver-on-sky row the catwalk keeps its middle at all five sizes, because the bar is ink and the body is silver. The 16 px number is a third of the 32 px one, which is antialiasing thinning a 1.3 px bar, but it stays well over the threshold.
  • The gradient is safe. The flat-silver controls are not better at 16 px in any measurable way and not visibly better either; the gradient starts paying from 32 up. Choose it on taste, not on legibility.
  • His own WT-TALL-3 aspect still costs 16 px, in color as in monochrome: the two legs and the riser fall inside three pixel columns and fuse. The fitted tower is the one that holds.
  • On the sink reading, the ground and the fill do the work, not the outline. The same WT-WIDE paths, silver on sky, read as a vessel outdoors; nothing was redrawn.
  • For the maskable pair we would put forward ic-mask-tall3fit-overcast: it needs only 0.80 rather than 0.74, its ground is the cloudy sky he asked for, and it is the one candidate whose catwalk still has a middle after the crop.
