# Virtual pipe: is there precedent for a flag? (Tom, 2026-11-06; Mary, 2026-11-08)

Public documentation only. No software was decompiled or probed. Every line carries a tag.

## What the others do

1. **EPANET 2.2** has no connector element and no flag. A pump or valve is a LINK between two nodes. - CITED. EPANET FAQ suggests a "short length of large diameter pipe" after a Pressure Sustaining Valve at a tank inlet; says a check valve is "a short length of pipe whose status is set to CV". https://epanet-manual.readthedocs.io/en/latest/12_questions.html . Nothing there on zero-length or dummy pipes (stated by the page fetch; not exhaustively searched).
2. **EPANET's own one-field-changes-meaning precedent.** - CITED. Pipe "Initial Status: Determines whether the pipe is initially open, closed, or contains a check valve. If a check valve is specified then the flow direction in the pipe will always be from the Start node to the End node." https://epanet-manual.readthedocs.io/en/latest/6_objects.html . So EPANET already turns an ordinary pipe into a different device through one enumerated field, not a new link type. Length stays "required", so a stand-in short pipe is still a real pipe.
3. **WaterGEMS/WaterCAD.** - CITED (secondary, search summary only; Bentley page redirected on fetch, so not read in full): community answers say a pump is linked to a reservoir by a short pipe, large diameter, short user-defined length; a tutorial copy uses 0.01 m. https://communities.bentley.com/products/hydraulics___hydrology/f/haestad-hydraulics-and-hydrology-forum/238600/submersible-pumps/742794 . Convention in numbers, no flag found.
4. **WaterGEMS EXPORT to EPANET** - CITED (search summary): connector pipes it generates are "1 foot in length and 99 inches in diameter, in order to minimize the import of headloss". https://communities.bentley.com/products/hydraulics___hydrology/w/hydraulics_and_hydrology__wiki/45050/missing-pipes-when-exporting-to-epanet . This is the closest precedent to our .inp export: a vendor writes a short wide stand-in on export. They chose short AND wide; we propose 0.01 m only.
5. **InfoWorks WS Pro** - CITED. Pumps and valves are link types; imported regulators "have zero length and coincident connecting nodes", and an "Expand Short Links" tool lengthens links below a stated value. https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2026/ENU/WSPro-Help/files/GUID-B310251F-9D51-4B6A-967E-96D404D95507.htm ; https://help2.innovyze.com/infoworkswspro/Content/HTML/WS/a_links.htm . Zero length is an accepted, ordinary state there, with no flag; the model also names the awkwardness (hard to see on the map).
6. **InfoWater Pro** - CITED (search summary): valves are drawn as node-like elements; for the EPANET engine it creates hidden junctions either side of the valve. https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/ENU/INFWP-UserGuide/files/GUID-F498BD5A-577D-45B0-A3CC-5793DCE67E80.htm . A hidden-element approach, the opposite of Tom's transparency.
7. **Synergi Water, KYPipe/Pipe2000.** NOT FOUND. I searched for "dummy pipe", "zero length", pump/valve element docs; no public passage turned up. Do not claim anything about them.
8. **epanet-js and other open tools.** NOT FOUND. No issue or doc on zero/minimum length or a connector flag surfaced. (An epanet-rtx issue on "check valve as valve or pipe" exists; unopened.)
9. **No tool found** has a boolean "is dummy/virtual" property on a pipe. - SPECULATION, from absence in the searches above; one more pass in WaterGEMS's own help might contradict it.

## The three options against that evidence

- **A, a flag (`virtual: true`).** No precedent found. Cost Tom named is real: a property on every pipe, in every table, copy, scenario and .inp comment. Its one merit: it survives a length edit.
- **B, stated length 0.** Nearest to practice: InfoWorks stores zero-length links as a plain state; WaterGEMS users type a tiny length; EPANET-style vendors write a short stand-in on export. It is transparent (the engineer sees "0" and knows the math), needs no new property, and round-trips through our .inp as a 0.01 m pipe. Risks: a real pipe whose length is accidentally 0 becomes lossless silently (SPECULATION: mitigate with a diagnostic note, not a refusal, per "trust the user's own work"); the export stand-in must be wide as well as short if 0.01 m of the pipe's own diameter adds visible loss (SPECULATION: 0.01 m at a small diameter and high flow is negligible, but Bentley chose 99 in; test before choosing). Volume is zero by construction, so no volume model is needed. Import of a .inp-written 0.01 m pipe will not come back as 0 unless the `;ASSEMBLY` comment says so (SPECULATION).
- **C, a word in the diameter field.** No precedent. It makes a numeric field hold text, breaks unit conversion, sorting, and the "supplied number and computed one never share a field" rule (OBSERVED: CLAUDE.md, Unit Sets). EPANET's Status CV shows an enumerated switch is acceptable, but on a text-bearing Status field, not on a numeric one.

## Recommendation

**B.** Virtual = length 0 as stated, shown plainly. It is Tom's transparency instinct, carries the precedent of InfoWorks (zero length is a normal state) and of the vendor stand-in on export, and adds no property. If Tom wants a guard, show a status-bar note on any length-0 pipe, never a block. A is the fallback if length 0 must be distinguishable from "forgot to type a length" (it cannot be, under B). C is the weakest.

Not tested: our solver's length-0 behaviour (Tom stated it gives zero resistance; I did not verify). Searches were summary-level; the Bentley pages redirected to a login-wall service-now site and were not read in full.
