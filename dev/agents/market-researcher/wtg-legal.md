# Could we get in trouble for writing .wtg / .wtg.sqlite files? (Mary, 2026-10-23)

NOT LEGAL ADVICE. I am a market researcher, not a lawyer. Several legal sources below were read only through search summaries and are marked so; none of the case opinions was read in full. Verify before relying.

## Bottom line
Low risk overall, concentrated in two places: (1) what Tom himself did with his licence, and (2) any use of the word WaterGEMS/WaterCAD/Bentley beyond a plain compatibility statement. Writing a file format is not what the EULA's reverse-engineering clause is aimed at. The cheapest risk reduction is also the cheapest feature decision: Bentley already imports EPANET .inp, so the .wtg writer is not needed for a user to reach WaterGEMS.

## 1. The EULA (current public version)
- CITED. https://www.bentley.com/en/legal/eula/ (English version controls; version date 2023-10-16 per search summary). Read via WebFetch summary, not a lawyer's reading.
- Art. 1 s.7 "LIMITATIONS ON REVERSE ENGINEERING": "You may not decode, reverse engineer, reverse assemble, reverse compile, or otherwise translate the Software." Allowed where applicable law expressly permits it, with 30 days' prior written notice to Bentley.
- CITED. "Software" is defined as "the Bentley software and associated online or electronic documentation that accompanies this EULA" (plus media, internet services, test software). Sample files, templates and DATA are not mentioned. The verbs (decode, assemble, compile, translate) are program verbs. The natural reading is the program, not a file the program wrote. SPECULATION: Bentley could argue "decode ... the Software" reaches studying its output; the text does not say so, and ambiguity is construed against the drafter in most US states (general contract rule, not looked up).
- CITED. s.18 "NO EXTENSION OF CAPABILITIES": "You may develop your own applications that interoperate or integrate with the Software." You may not "extend the Software to enable or unlock capabilities of the Software not specifically identified by Bentley" (reason given: pricing by capability). A file writer that produces ordinary models unlocks nothing; but a writer that produced something a licence tier does not allow (for example more pipes than the user's edition) could be argued to cross this line. SPECULATION.
- CITED. s.2 documentation "for internal, non-commercial reference purposes only." s.3 Bentley keeps all right in the Software. No clause on file formats, derivative works, ownership of user data, benchmarking, or competing products appears in the text I got.
- CITED. s.33: for USA and Canada, Pennsylvania law, exclusive federal court in Philadelphia. UCITA and CISG excluded. Acceptance is by installing or using.
- Binds whom? CITED: "a legal agreement between you (an individual or single entity)" and Bentley, accepted by downloading, installing or using. So it binds Tom (and Hawsedc if the licence is the firm's), and every licensee who uses the export. It does NOT bind a visitor who has no copy of WaterGEMS, nor the public who read Zenodo files. SPECULATION (general contract principle: privity): Bentley has no contract with them. Tom's exposure is therefore personal and about HOW HE OBTAINED his test files, not about what visitors do.
- Caution: the EULA text can differ from the one that came with Tom's installed copy (older or product-specific versions; the 2016 government version is a different document, https://www.carahsoft.com/buy/gsa-schedule-contracts/approved-csas/docFileDownload/286450/24221/72047619/0). Check the EULA.pdf in his install folder. Tom's own quoted phrase "Reverse engineering is prohibited" matches s.7.

## 2. Law
- US contract overrides fair use: Bowers v. Baystate, 320 F.3d 1317 (Fed. Cir. 2003): a shrinkwrap ban on reverse engineering was enforced as a contract despite fair use and copyright preemption arguments. CITED via secondary sources only: https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/publications/shrink-wrap-licenses-and-reverse-engineering-april-16-2003 and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowers_v._Baystate_Technologies,_Inc. Lesson: "it is fair use" is NOT a defence to a EULA clause. That is why Tom's decision to honour the clause is the right call: the contract is the real exposure, not copyright. (Massachusetts law there; Bentley's is Pennsylvania; other circuits split. SPECULATION on how Pennsylvania/3rd Circuit would go.)
- Sega v. Accolade (9th Cir. 1992) and Sony v. Connectix (9th Cir. 2000): intermediate copying to get at interoperability elements is fair use. CITED via search summary (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limitations_on_exclusive_rights:_Computer_programs; https://cases.justia.com/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/09-16044/60/0.pdf for context). Helps against a copyright claim, not a contract claim (see Bowers). Arizona is in the 9th Circuit, but the EULA picks Philadelphia.
- DMCA 1201(f) (interoperability exemption for circumventing an access control): CITED via summary, not primary text. Not engaged here as far as I can see: a .wtg.sqlite is plain SQLite, and nothing indicates encryption or an access control (dev/bentley-interop.md lists encryption as "unknown"; the later spike opened and refilled the files with ordinary SQLite tools, OBSERVED in the wtg-export.md integrity checks). If any column proved encrypted, stop and ask a lawyer.
- EU: SAS Institute v World Programming, C-406/10 (2012). CITED via search summary (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAS_Institute_Inc_v_World_Programming_Ltd; https://www.fieldfisher.com/en/insights/sas-institute-inc-v-world-programming-limited): neither a program's functionality nor "the format of data files" is protected expression; a lawful user may observe, study and test the program to find its ideas. On remittal (Arnold J) the Learning Edition use was outside the contract but still not copyright infringement. Software Directive art. 5(3), 6 and 8-style rules cannot be removed by contract in the EU (secondary source). Note: the case protects studying the program's function; art. 6 decompilation is about program code, which is not what we did. This helps EU users and EU developers; it does not rewrite a US licensee's Pennsylvania contract. Tom is US-based.
- Net: file formats and interoperability are broadly protected from copyright claims in both systems. The remaining risk is CONTRACT (EULA) and, for anything copied, copyright in the copied rows/schema.

## 3. Trademark
- The product name must be used to say what the export is for. Ninth Circuit nominative fair use (New Kids on the Block, 1992): (1) product not readily identifiable without the mark, (2) use only as much as needed, (3) nothing suggesting sponsorship or endorsement. CITED via summary: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nominative_use and https://ktslaw.com/insights/alert/2017/1/circuit-split-remains-scotus-passes-on-defining-nominative-fair-use. Other circuits differ (circuit split, same source). I did not verify Toyota v. Tabari or any "compatible with" case.
- Safe wording (SPECULATION, standard practice): plain text, no logos, no Bentley fonts or colours; "Export to a WaterGEMS project file" or "Compatible with Bentley WaterGEMS and WaterCAD"; and one line where it appears: "WaterGEMS and WaterCAD are trademarks of Bentley Systems, Incorporated. This project is not affiliated with or endorsed by Bentley." Do not put the name in a domain, product name, or logo. Avoid "certified", "official", "works with all versions".
- OBSERVED, our own rule: dev/positioning.md says no competitor, and expressly no live commercial trademark (WaterCAD, WaterGEMS, Bentley), appears in a title, meta description, tagline, menu item or headline (Task 296 and the voluntary extension). A menu row labelled "Export to WaterGEMS" would collide with our own rule, not with the law. Tom would have to grant an exception, like the EPANET one.
- Cautionary precedent: Autodesk v. Open Design Alliance. CITED via search summary: https://www.rttnews.com/1265276/autodesk-agrees-to-settle-dwg-trademark-disputes-with-open-design-alliance-update.aspx and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.dwg. Autodesk sued in 2006 over the TrustedDWG watermark (a trademark claim, not a format claim), got a TRO in nine days, settled 2007 mostly on Autodesk's terms; in 2010 ODA dropped "DWG" trademarks, but Autodesk said it did not preclude interoperable software or the .dwg extension. Lesson: vendors fight over names and over writing the vendor's own trust marks, and interoperability itself survived. Do not write any Bentley watermark/signature string into files. The writer should not copy a "created by WaterGEMS" version stamp as if Bentley wrote it. (The spike's template copy does that by construction: SPECULATION that this is a small issue, worth asking about.)

## 5. Risk ranking (lowest to highest, with who bears it)
1. LOWEST: Using Tom's licence to save test files. Saving a project is the ordinary use the software exists for. The clause bans decoding the Software; opening the SQLite output with a SQLite viewer is studying data. The residual argument is that systematic study of output to learn the schema is "translating" the Software; Bowers shows contract wins over fair use, but the text does not say it. Risk: low, personal to Tom, damage = licence termination (s.20) or a letter, not a lawsuit-sized loss. Keep records that he looked at files, never the program, DLLs, memory, or network traffic (the spike already says so).
2. LOW: Public naming with nominative wording and a non-affiliation line. The legal risk is small; the larger conflict is our own positioning rule.
3. LOW-MEDIUM: Shipping the writer publicly. Visitors are not bound by the EULA; no copyright in file format ideas (SAS, CITED). Medium points: (a) a file that opens but is subtly wrong carries our name into Bentley's support queue and a reputation cost (SPECULATION; dev/bentley-interop.md already warns "worse than a refusal"); (b) s.18: do not unlock tier-limited features (element count limits).
4. MEDIUM (design-dependent): Template provenance.
   - User-supplied blank project (design in wtg-export.md): lowest. The schema and default rows never enter our repository or our source; the user creates it under their own licence. Cleanest answer to the copyright question (rows are Bentley's authorship or at least compilation, and copying them would be reproduction).
   - Template bundled in our repository (what the spike uses, Tom's fixture): do NOT ship it. It would be a Bentley-generated file (default rows, version stamp, schema DDL) redistributed by us with a GPL licence we cannot grant. The spike correctly keeps it out of the repo.
   - Fresh schema written from scratch: avoids copying but requires reconstruction from files; weaker on both correctness and the "studied output" optics.
5. HIGHEST (not in the plan; do not do): looking inside the program or DLLs, circumventing any encryption, hosting Bentley's template, using Bentley logos, using the name in a product name or domain, or implying approval.

## 6. Recommendations, cheapest first
1. Free: keep the line already drawn. Only files from the program's ordinary Save, Zenodo CC BY and MIT samples. Do not look at the executables. Keep a dated note (the existing wtg-export.md "Licence line, kept" is that note; add the date and EULA version Tom read).
2. Free: Tom should read the EULA that actually shipped with his installed copy (the one he accepted), especially any Annex for WaterGEMS (the web version may differ), and note whether it mentions data files, "derivative", or "competitive". 10 minutes.
3. Free: ask Bentley before launch. The tidiest lawful answer: write to Bentley's developer relations / OpenFlows (the Bentley Communities forum or sales rep) and describe the export as an EPANET interoperability feature. Silence is not consent, but a reply either way is evidence. Risk: they say no (then we know and can drop it with no loss). SPECULATION on how they'd reply.
4. Free: ship user-supplied template only; refuse unknown schema versions; never include Bentley-written bytes in the repo or the JS.
5. Free: wording. If it ships: "Export a WaterGEMS-compatible project" in plain type, the trademark line, no logo, and get Tom's explicit exception to the Task 296 rule first (or keep the label generic: "Export project file (Bentley)" is still the name; the safer generic is "Export to .wtg.sqlite" which names a file type, not a product). The EPANET `.inp` export remains the headline and the route Bentley itself recommends.
6. Free: product framing. Make clear in the dialog that the file is not tested or supported by Bentley, to keep failures from being attributed to Bentley, and state what is not carried (the spike's report already does this).
7. Worth a lawyer (about one hour of a US IP/software-licensing attorney; typically a few hundred to low thousands of dollars, SPECULATION on price): before the writer ships publicly or before a Windows executable that bundles it, ask three things: does EULA s.7 as drafted cover studying a file the licensee saved; does a user-supplied-template design remove our copyright exposure; is the proposed trademark wording adequate. Also worth it immediately if Bentley contacts Tom, because the EULA names Pennsylvania courts and a licensed individual is personally exposed. Not worth a lawyer: the choice of whether to name the product (use the plain wording and our own positioning).
8. Strategic check (SPECULATION): the marginal gain over .inp is mainly what .inp cannot carry (scenarios, patterns detail), and Bentley's INP import loses VSP and other elements by its own account (CITED above). If that loss is the only gain, the writer's legal and reputational cost may exceed its value; the market-research question is which users actually need it. I have no evidence of demand, and have not looked for it in this note.
