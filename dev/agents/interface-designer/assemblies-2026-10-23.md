# Ida: assemblies and expert levels (2026-10-23)

## Position in one line
Draw an assembly as ONE symbol on a SHORT, FIXED-LENGTH DRAWN STUB, never two coincident nodes. Do not build expert levels now; if levels ever ship, make them change NOTHING about what is drawn and only how much help is volunteered.

## How it reads on the map (my focus)
1. Coincident nodes are a hierarchy failure before they are a data problem. Two nodes at one point draw one circle: the reader sees one thing and the model holds two. OBSERVED: nearest-node finder `nearestNodeNearScreen` (js/looped-network.js:9992-10000) uses `dPx <= bestPx`, so on an exact tie the LATER node in doc.nodes wins every time. The upstream node would be unreachable by click, hover, and touch. That is the answer to 4a's "how do we change UI paradigms": we would need a new tie-breaker (cycle on repeat click, as Illustrator/Figma "select behind" does, CITED from memory) plus a way to show two things are there. SPECULATION: that is a new modal paradigm to teach, for a case the stub avoids entirely.
2. Stub: the pump or valve is a link with a glyph at its midpoint (already how a pump or valve is drawn; OBSERVED js/lpn-profile.js:27 treats it as lengthless). Give the assembly a minimum DRAWN length in screen pixels (not world units) so zooming out never collapses it to a dot, and the two end nodes stay separately pickable. Drawn length is not model length: the stored length stays 0 or the 4-6 default. SPECULATION, needs a harness measure of label collision (js/lpn-collide.js) at a 50 px stub.
3. Labels: one label for the assembly (the pump's name), placed beside the glyph; the two end nodes get no label of their own while "managed". Their auto names ("P-1 in", "P-1 out") appear in Properties and on hover only. Two labels on two coincident nodes would overprint; that alone kills the coincident design. OBSERVED collision code exists (js/lpn-collide.js) but resolves distinct anchors, not identical ones. SPECULATION on the fix.
4. Selection: click the glyph selects the assembly (drag moves all three together, Tom's benefit 3). A second click, or Tab, or clicking an end dot, selects the node alone. This is the group/enter-group convention of Illustrator, Figma, PowerPoint (CITED from memory). It answers 4a without a "Manage as assembly" checkbox: the unit is movable as a whole by default and its parts are reachable by one more click.
5. Position on 4a/4c: DO NOT make the assembly immutable and DO NOT keep a checkbox. A checkbox is a persistent mode shown on every pump; most are never touched (same lesson as my override-marker entry, OBSERVED journal 2026-10-12: a permanently unticked box on every row is noise). Offer "Explode" as a verb in the context menu and Edit menu: it removes the grouping and leaves plain EPANET parts. That is 4c, and it also answers 2. Cost: one string, translated once. SPECULATION that no one will want to re-assemble; an Undo covers the mistake.
6. 4b, differently at different levels: no. A symbol that changes with a setting makes screenshots, support and teaching unreliable, and the reader cannot tell which they are looking at. The same drawing for everyone; levels may change only what the page tells you, never what it shows. SPECULATION, with the support argument OBSERVED in the three 2026-09-09 sessions (the people did not know where things were; a variable layout would be worse).

## Menu/toolbar (question 1)
Two rows in the menu is right (words, durable, translatable). The toolbar cycle button is the part I'd reject: a cycle control hides its state (OBSERVED: toolbar is icon-only by ruling, and its tip is the primary teacher, js/Calculators.lib.js:276-290), and you cannot tell which of two modes the button is in without pressing it. Better: the pump and valve toolbar buttons place an ASSEMBLY (the new default, the thing Tom wants easy); the plain link is menu-only (Draw > Pump link). This costs no toolbar slot and no new state. Hold Alt for the plain one, matching the existing Alt-bypass of the magnet (js/looped-network.js ~10030), though Alt is invisible, so the menu row is the primary route.

## Expert levels as a frame
- Where: Help's first item is wrong. Help is where people go when stuck; a setting there is found by the people who least need it and missed by those who do (the 2026-09-09 result: people did not see the menu bar at all). Put it in Settings, or better: not as a named level at all.
- Risk of an unknown mode: severe. Someone who inherits a machine in Expert sees no tips and cannot know why. If a level exists it must show in the chrome all the time (a word beside the menu, translated 27 times) or it is a trap. SPECULATION.
- What changes beyond tip delay: very little should. Delay already exists as a tunable (EngCalcs.tipShowDelay, data-ec-tip-delay; OBSERVED Calculators.lib.js:295). Stopper dialogs for Tutorial are a hazard: a confirm before every action is exactly what trains people to click through. A coach mark that points at the control (non-blocking, dismissible, once) does the teaching with no blocking.
- Existing Basic mode is scenarios-only (js/looped-network.js:5401); reusing the word "Basic" for a second, different thing breaks "one name, one job" (CLAUDE.md). Rename one.
- My recommendation: do not build levels. Two real needs are covered cheaper: a first-run hint, and a tip-delay preference. Revisit if testers ask.

## For my wish list
1. Before any assembly build, measure with the harness: node pick on two coincident nodes (expect later-wins), label overprint, and a 50 px stub at zoom-out.
2. Whole-assembly selection with "click again to reach a part" (group/enter-group convention), plus Explode in Edit and the context menu.
3. Toolbar pump/valve place assemblies; plain link in the menu only (and Alt); no cycle control.
4. Do not ship expert levels without a permanent on-screen indicator; prefer a tip-delay setting in Settings.
