# Label placement, round 6 (2026-10-05): bent pipes, valves, big networks, and the drop order

**Builders must not read this file or `dev/label-trials/`.** It scores with the judges' tools
(`dev/lpn-spike/label-bench/judges/`).

Tasks 741 and 539. Tom's answers to the round-5 proposals (`dev/label-placement-rules.md`, "Round 5
proposals, his answers"): *yes* to bent pipes, valves, large networks and fresh secret scenes every
round; *yes* (2026-10-05) to the reworded R1, *"Hide a label only because there is no room for it
on screen. Never hide it because of how many labels are already showing."*; and on dense zooms:
*"Since the user decides drop order (meaning they really want to see what they asked for), it might
be best to prioritize more labels with a single value left than less labels with more values
left."* That last is **not a rule**; this round measures it.

Part 1, the protocol, is committed before any placer is scored on a round-6 scene (git history is
the timestamp). Part 2 is written afterwards. A change to Part 1 after scoring starts is listed
under "Deviations" in Part 2, never edited in silently.

## Part 1: Protocol (pre-registered)

### 1.1 Questions

1. Do bent pipes and valves cause the breaks round 5 found (D's labels over bent pipes, master's
   labels over valve symbols), on networks nobody has seen, when each is switched on and off on the
   SAME network?
2. Does a layout's cost follow the screen, not the network (R10 as Tom confirmed it), out to
   20,000 nodes? How many views take longer than his one second?
3. Do the placers keep R1 as he worded it: is a hidden label ever hidden while there was room, and
   is one ever hidden because of how many others are showing?
4. **His drop-order observation:** on a crowded view, does showing more labels with only their
   most-wanted value left beat showing fewer labels with more values, and by how much, in what
   each shows?
5. Does round 5's order of the placers hold on fresh seeds?
6. (For Task 539, descriptive.) Does the widest gap between the pipes at a node (his ranked gap
   list, R-079, step 1 of finding `spot_prime`) point at the free ground?

### 1.2 Materials

**Placers**: the same five as round 5, at the same commits (files unmodified since; sha256, first
12 characters): master (this branch's `js/looped-network.js`, replayed from the page as recorded
by `extract.js`), A `3483bfd6` `e14958e44ebc`, B `c5905432` `d75fee62e228`, C `9a790a3e`
`a21a307a8ad7`, D `129b63c4` `9924a126a244`. **Master here is this branch's copy, about 900
commits behind today's master**; it is the round-5 baseline, not the shipped page of today.

**The generator** (`generator.js`) gains two factors, each on its own seeded stream drawn for every
link whatever is decided, so switching one on leaves the rest of the network as it was (round-5
networks reproduce exactly; checked on 48 of them):

- **bends**: `none`, or `many`: in every family, 60% of the pipes longer than half a block curve
  through 1 to 3 vertices on a jittered arc up to 0.3 of their length off the chord.
- **valves**: `none`, or `many`: one link in 12 (longer than half a block) is a valve (TCV, open:
  the symbol on the map, no hydraulic effect). Round 5 had one in 150.

**Fresh seeds**: 61-64 for E1, 61-62 for E2 (round 5 used 1-6). Fields are Novato's (node ID, P,
Qb, Z; pipe ID, Q, V), text 12 px, "Draw link labels along the link line" on, IDs 'epanet' style
(2 to 5 digits). Five views per set: the first puts the median nearest-neighbour distance at the
set's density, then 1.5x, 2x, 3x, 4x about the same node.

**The drop order** in every scene is Novato's: node elevation, then demand, then pressure last;
pipe velocity, then flow last. So a label's **most-wanted value** (the last to drop) is P for a
node and Q for a pipe. The bench never drops an ID (`contract.js`); the app today ships with the ID
second to drop (Tom's table, R-326), so the app's own label down to one value shows P= or Q= alone.

### 1.3 Design

|---|---|---|---|
| E0 | the public bench (Net1, Net2, Net3, Novato x2) | 5 | all five |
| E1 | family (4) x seed (61-64) x density (28, 44 px) x variant: plain, bent, valved, both | 128 | all five (n = 1000) |

| E3 | the drop-order arms on E1's 'both' sets: `two` (each label asked for its ID and its most-wanted value only) and `one` (its most-wanted value only), against E1's `full` | 32 x 2 | A-D |

`matrix.js` is this table as code. 161 scene sets, 1,049 cells. Every cell runs as the bench runs a
placer (`runBench()`: a fresh instance per set, 3000 ms of idle before the first view, 250 ms
between views, `prev` passed). One child process per cell, a 30-minute limit; a cell that fails or
times out is recorded as such and counts against its placer. At most six cells at once, in a
seeded random order, on jasmine (24 threads, 60 GB; other sessions' check_all suites may run at the
same time, so times compare placers, not machines). Work files on disk in
`/home/haws/label-trials-work/r6`, not the RAM-backed `/tmp`.

**E3 is the measured comparison Tom asked for, not a rule.** The arms ask each builder the same
question with less to place; which arm is "best" is his call. What the arms isolate: given the
same placer and the same view, what a layout gains in labels and in most-wanted values, and what
it loses in other values and in IDs, when the request itself is cut down to the single value the
user ranked last to drop.

### 1.4 Metrics (per view, summed over a set's views; the set is the unit)

Round 5's, unchanged: breaks (N1, N3, N4, N5, invalid), labels shown %, values shown %, G missed
room %, leader on leader per 100 shown labels, Tom-weighted cost per label, time per layout
(median, max), churn, R11 rows lost on zoom-in. New:

- **Top value %**: labels showing their most-wanted value / labels requested (always against the
  FULL request, in every arm). **ID %**: labels showing their ID / labels requested. In the arms,
  values % is also against the full request's values.
- **R1, no room** (`judges/r1.js`): of the labels hidden, those for which free ground within 3 rows
  (the judges' `room.js` search) could hold the ID row, by node and pipe labels.
- **R1, not by count** (`judges/r1.js` `countProbe()`), at the 1x and 2x views of every E1 and E2
  builder cell: the view laid out afresh, again unchanged (control), and with each half of the
  screen's labels not asked for in turn; a label more than a quarter of the screen beyond the cut
  that was hidden in the full layout and shown in the half one was hidden by count ("revived").
- **Views over one second** (Tom's R10 bound): views whose layout took more than 1,000 ms.
- **Gaps** (`metrics6.js`, descriptive): at nodes with 2+ pipes, the rank (widest first) of the gap
  between pipes that holds (a) a shown node label's direction from its node, and (b) for a node
  label hidden with room, the best-ranked gap holding that free ground; with the baseline share a
  random direction would land in the widest gap.

### 1.5 Hypotheses (stated before scoring)

- **H1 (bends).** D breaks N1 more on bent networks than on the same networks straight (paired
  sign test, p < 0.05); A, B and C break on none.
- **H2 (valves).** Master breaks more with valves than without (paired, p < 0.05); A-D are
  unaffected (no set with a break that the valve-free twin lacks).
- **H3 (bends cost space).** Bent pipes lower labels shown % for every placer (paired, Holm
  p < 0.05 over the five placers).
- **H4 (R10, scale).** For each builder, the median layout time at 20,000 nodes is at most twice
  that at 5,000 nodes on the paired network at the same density (a cost that follows the screen
  would be about 1x; one that follows the network, about 4x). Reported with it: views over 1 s.
- **H5 (R1).** Each builder revives at most 5% of the far hidden labels beyond its control flips
  (none hides by count); each builder hides some labels with room (at least 1% of those it hides),
  i.e. R1's first half is broken by every builder somewhere.
- **H6 (drop order).** At 28 px, for every builder, the `one` arm shows the most-wanted value on
  more labels than the `full` arm (paired sign test, p < 0.05), and fewer values in all than the
  `full` arm. At 44 px the top-value gain is smaller than at 28 px.
- **H8 (the round-5 recheck).** On the 21 round-5 specs where D broke N1, with real vertices D
  breaks on fewer sets than it did (sign test over the 21, p < 0.05). If so, round 5's "D breaks on
  bent pipes" was the generator's defect, not D's; if not, D's fault stands on real bends.
- **H7 (replication).** The placers' order on labels %, values % and G miss in E1 'plain' matches
  round 5's E1 (n <= 1000), Kendall tau >= 0.6 on each.

### 1.6 Analysis

`analyse.js` in the round folder, deterministic: paired differences on the same network (and the
same density and other factor), mean with a 95% bootstrap interval (10,000 resamples, seeded) and
an exact two-sided sign test; Holm's correction over the five placers within an outcome where H3
says so. A hypothesis is supported when every test it names goes its way at the stated p.

Rule verdicts follow from the outcomes as in round 5: **supported** when the placers that keep a
rule do better on what it names, **contradicted** when keeping it costs what it claims to save,
**underdetermined** when the data cannot tell.

### 1.7 Pilot, disclosed

Before this protocol was committed: one E1-like set (suburban, n = 1000, 28 px, seed 99, bent and
valved) was extracted with master (11 s) and scored with C and master to test the scorer. After
the vertex fix below, at 1x: C showed 1,097 of 1,982 labels, each with its ID, 57 with P or Q;
master showed 331, every one P or Q **without its ID** (the branch's master already drops the ID
first), and broke N1 39 times, on valve symbols. C's count probe revived 11 of 443 far hidden labels
with no control flips. (Before the fix C showed 335.) H5's 5% was written knowing this one view. One 20,000-node extraction was
run to size E2 (seed 99, not in the design): 8.5 minutes and 11 GB for one process, so E2's
extraction runs at most two at a time. The generator hung at 10,000 nodes (four-digit IDs ran out);
it now uses five digits past 9,990 elements, which changes no round-5 network.

**The pilot found a defect that voids part of round 5.** On the 20,000-node pilot A and C showed no
label at all. The cause: the generator wrote a pipe's vertices as `[x, y]` pairs, and the app
stores and reads `{x, y}` (`linkPointList()`), so every bent pipe ran through a vertex at no
position (NaN in the page, `null` in the scene file; anything doing arithmetic on it reads it as 0,
the corner of the screen). In round 5 that was 15% of the suburban pipes and 5% of the downtown
ones; at "many" bends, most of the screen. The generator now writes `{x, y}`. Consequences for
round 5, to be tested here rather than assumed: **its "bent pipes" finding (H1: D breaks N1 on
bent pipes) rested on pipes that were not bent but broken**, which is what E4 and H8 check; every
placer saw the same broken scenes, so its comparisons between placers stand; L-Town and C-Town have
no vertices at all, so round 5 had no real bent pipe anywhere.

## Part 2: Results and analysis (written after scoring, 2026-10-05)

Every number below comes from `round-6-2026-10-05/results.md`, which `analyse.js` generates from
the raw per-view results in `round-6-2026-10-05/raw/`; `sets.csv` holds every (placer, set, arm)
aggregate. One table (R14 on bent pipes, 2.5) is exploratory, not pre-registered, and says so.

### 2.1 What ran

1,074 cells (the design's 1,049 plus E0's 25), 5,370 views, on jasmine. **No cell failed or timed
out.** 161 scene sets extracted with no failure; a 20,000-node set took about 4 minutes to extract.

### 2.2 Deviations from the protocol

1. The vertex defect (1.7) was fixed in the same commit as the protocol, before any scene was
   extracted for the design.
2. E2 (the large networks) was scored by a second runner, three cells at a time, beside the first
   runner's three, once its scenes existed (`score-all.js --exp E2`, an option added for it), so E2
   cells were not shuffled in among the others. E2's times are compared only with each other.
3. After the protocol, the public bench gained a scene with bent pipes and valves (`bent-valves`,
   on Tom's yes). E0 here is the five sets the protocol names.

No hypothesis, metric, threshold or test was changed.

### 2.3 The hypotheses

- **H8 (round 5 recheck): supported, and it changes round 5.** On the 21 round-5 specs where D
  broke N1 (51 breaks), extracted again with real vertices, D breaks **none** (21 of 21 sets
  fewer, sign p < 0.001). **Round 5's finding "D breaks on bent pipes" was our generator's defect,
  not D's.**
- **H1 (bends cause breaks): contradicted.** With real bends, no builder breaks anything: A, B, C
  and D have zero breaks on all 128 E1 sets and all 12 E2 sets.
- **H2 (valves): supported.** Master writes labels over valve symbols: 5,698 N1 breaks with valves
  against 34 without, on the same networks (64 of 64 pairs worse). The builders are untouched.
- **H3 (bends cost space): mostly supported.** Bends cost master 2.9 points of labels shown, B 1.5,
  C 0.5, A 0.4 (Holm p < 0.001 each); D nothing measurable (-0.05, p 0.90). Small for every
  builder.
- **H4 (cost follows the screen to 20,000 nodes): not supported, but inside his bound.** On the same
  screen, a builder's median layout grows 2.4x (A) to 3.3x (C) from 5,000 to 20,000 nodes, so the
  cost still follows the network. In absolute terms it does not matter yet: medians 82-117 ms at
  20,000 nodes, the worst single view 0.8 s (A), and **no view over one second** in E2. In E1
  (1,000 nodes), master took over a second on 495 of 640 views; A on 14 of 640; B, C, D never. Part
  of the growth is the contract itself: it hands a placer every node and pipe, on screen or not.
- **H5 (R1): supported, in both halves.** *Not by count:* A and B revive no far label (0 of 24,492
  and 27,414); C revives 2.5% and D 3.3%, under the 5% line, with no flips in the control run (the
  likely cause is a per-layout work budget, which does depend on how many labels there are). D also
  hid 1,174 far labels when half the screen was emptied. *Only for lack of room:* every placer
  hides labels that had room for their ID within three rows: C 29% of the labels it hides, A 42%,
  B 48%, D 58%, master 99%. No builder was ever given Tom's sentence.
- **H6 (the drop-order arms): his observation holds, and the trade is not the one we expected.**
  Asked for the most-wanted value alone (`one`), every builder shows it on 33 to 47 more labels in
  100 than when asked for the whole label (16 of 16 networks each, both densities). The price is
  5 to 12 fewer labels in 100 (a value like "P=65.65" is wider than an ID like "8315") and no IDs.
  At 28 px `one` also shows **more** values in all (8 to 15 points more), not fewer as predicted;
  at 44 px it is mixed. Asking for the ID plus that one value (`two`) changes almost nothing:
  labels the same, the most-wanted value up 0 to 4 points. **What the builders do at a crowded view
  is keep the ID and drop every value, because the bench tells them the ID is never dropped.** At
  28 px they show P or Q on 17-27% of the labels asked for; master, which drops the ID first as
  Tom's table says, on 35%. The predicted "smaller gain at 44 px" held for A and C only.
- **H7 (round 5's order holds): partly.** The order on G (missed room) is identical (tau 1.0, C best,
  master worst) and on values shown nearly so (0.8); on labels shown it is not (0.4: A edges C). Round
  5's suburban and downtown scenes were damaged by the vertex defect, which lowered every placer's
  numbers there (C showed 335 of 1,982 labels on the damaged pilot, 1,097 on the repaired one).

### 2.6 For Task 539: how `spot_prime` is found (R-079, the ranked gap list)

The gaps between the pipes meeting at a node, ranked widest first, measured on every node label at
a node with two or more pipes (E1):

- **Where the free ground is.** For a label hidden although there was room, the widest gap held
  that room 52-66% of the time, the second 21-27%, the rest 13-21%. **So the widest gap alone, which
  is what the shipped code keeps, misses a third to a half of the free ground; the top two gaps
  hold 80-87% of it.**
- **Where the good placers put labels.** The builders put 55-61% of their node labels in the widest
  gap, against 47% by chance; master puts 79% there, and it is the placer that leaves the most room
  unused.

The answer to report back: step 1 of finding `spot_prime` is cheap and pays, **as a ranked list,
not a single winner**; a search over the top two gaps would find about five-sixths of the room a
plain search finds. Steps 2-3 (sizing `box_est` in the gap) remain unmeasured.

### 2.7 Threats to validity

- **The placers predate R1's new sentence**, and A and B predate R14; their R1 numbers are not
  failures against what they were told.
- **The generator is still ours**, and its first bends were wrong for a whole round before anyone
  saw it. A scene that draws nothing on screen is the cheapest check that would have caught it:
  the bench should refuse a scene with a non-numeric point (proposal 5).
- **Master is the branch's copy**, about 900 commits behind today's master.
- **No published network has a bent pipe**: L-Town and C-Town carry no vertices.
- **Legibility to a person is not measured.** Whether "P=65.65" without an ID is useful on a
  crowded map is Tom's judgement, not a number here.

## Proposals for Tom

Each is a yes or no. `dev/label-placement-rules.md` is unchanged until he answers.

1. **At a crowded view, should a label keep its most-wanted value (the last in your drop order, P or
   Q by default) rather than its ID, as your drop-order table already says?** Concretely: the bench
   would stop telling placers "the ID is never dropped" and hand them the ID in the drop order like
   any other value. Evidence (H6): told to keep only that value, every builder showed it on 33 to 47
   more labels in 100, at a cost of 5 to 12 fewer labels in 100 and no IDs. Today the builders keep
   bare IDs: at 28 px they show P or Q on only 17-27% of the labels asked for.
2. **Should the next builders be scored with R1's new sentence as a pass or fail?** It is in Part A
   now and the judges measure both halves. Every placer today hides labels that had room (29-58% of
   those it hides); none hides by count.
3. **Should we measure today's master on the new bent-and-valved scene, and file a defect if it
   still writes labels over valve symbols?** The branch's copy did so 5,698 times on 64 networks
   (34 without valves), and drew 11-13% of its pipe labels level on bent pipes where they had room to
   lie along them.
4. **For Task 539: should step 1 of the `spot_prime` hunt be the ranked list of a node's gaps,
   widest first, searched at least two deep?** The widest gap alone holds the free ground only
   52-66% of the time; the top two hold 80-87%.
5. **Should the bench refuse a scene with a missing or non-numeric coordinate?** Round 5 ran a whole
   round on pipes that pointed at the corner of the screen and nobody saw it; one check would have.

**Not a proposal, a correction:** round 5's "D breaks N1 on bent pipes" is withdrawn. It was our
generator's defect (H8); on real bends no builder breaks anything.
