## Part 1: Protocol (pre-registered)

### 1.1 Question

Does what we have learned about the placers on the public bench (EPA Net1, Net2, Net3, and Net3 on
the world map at Novato) hold on networks nobody tuned against: larger, denser, differently shaped
networks that run off the screen on every side? Where does each placer fail, and does Net3 hide
it? And which of Tom's rules (`dev/label-placement-rules.md`, Part A) does the evidence support,
contradict, or leave underdetermined?

Master is not a pure function the bench can call; its layouts are recorded from the real page
(`js/looped-network.js` on the node DOM stub) by `extract.js`, exactly as for the public scenes.

**Scenes.** The generator `dev/lpn-spike/label-bench/generator.js` (committed 2279a57e, ca79cb32)
makes a network as a project document from (family, n, seed), deterministically (mulberry32 over a
hash of the parameters; three separate streams so the same seed gives the same network at every
density and with every ID style). Four families:

- **grid**: a street grid, jittered by 0.2 of the spacing, 8% of links removed (never below two per
  node), trunk mains every fifth street;
- **tree**: rural branched mains from one source, no loops;
- **suburban**: random points at a minimum spacing, a minimum spanning tree, then a looping pass
  (30% of short candidate links, no crossings, no slivers under 25 degrees); 15% of pipes bent;
- **downtown**: 75% of points drawn from a tight Gaussian core, 25% spread wide, joined like
  suburban with 55% looping; so density varies within one view.

Every network has one reservoir (and a 24-inch main from it), one tank per 1000 nodes, a TCV on one
link in 150, terrain-shaped elevations and random demands, and is solved by the vendored EPANET
engine, so the pressure, demand, flow and velocity rows carry realistic text. The label fields are
Novato's (node: ID, P, Qb, Z; pipe: ID, flow, velocity), text 12 px, "Draw link labels along the
link line" on.

**Density** is set by the view, not the network: the first view puts the median nearest-neighbour
distance at `spacingPx` screen pixels, centred on the node nearest the centroid, and the set then
zooms 1.5x, 2x, 3x and 4x about the same node (five views per set). A 5000-node network at 44 px
runs about 20 screens wide: the infinite map.

**Published networks** (`dev/lpn-spike/label-bench/networks/`, licences there): L-Town (BattLeDIM,
782 junctions, CC BY 4.0) and C-Town (BATADAL, 388 junctions, CC BY 4.0), read by the app's own
`.inp` importer. Anytown, the Kentucky set and the other ASCE database networks are CC BY-NC and
are not committed; BWSN, E-Town, Richmond, Net6 and the Exeter set have no licence we could
confirm.

`matrix.js` in the round folder is this table as code. **Master is not run at n = 5000.** A pilot
(1.6) found its layout time grows with the whole network rather than the view (11 minutes at 2,500
nodes with 515 labels on screen), so its 5000-node scenes are extracted with master's collision pass
switched off (`extract.js` `noMaster`); the scene handed to the placers is byte-identical either way
(checked on the 2,500-node pilot scene).

Each placer runs as the bench runs it (`run.js runBench()`): a fresh instance per set, 3000 ms of
idle before the first view and 250 ms between views, the previous view passed as `prev`. One child
process per (placer, set), a 20-minute limit; a cell that fails or times out is recorded as such
and counts against the placer. Cells run two at a time in a seeded random order on one machine
(Intel i7-7500U, 2 cores / 4 threads, 12 GB, WSL2, node 24), so machine load falls evenly on every
placer; times are comparable between placers, not absolute.

### 1.4 Metrics

Per view, then summed over a set's five views (the set is the unit of analysis):

- **Breaks**: N1 + N3 + N4 + N5 + invalid placements (bench `score.js`). Must be zero.
- **Labels shown %** and **values shown %** (non-ID rows shown / requested).
- **G, missed room** (new, `metrics.js`): of the labels requested, those HIDDEN although free ground
  within 3 text rows could hold their ID row, plus those CUT (fewer rows than requested) although
  free ground within 3 rows could hold the whole label, in that same finished layout. The search is
  the judges' `room.js` exactly (15-degree steps, half-row radii, no symbol, label, Text, pipe; a
  straight leader through no other node and across no other leader); `metrics.js` indexes it on a
  grid and `selftest.js` proves agreement with `room.js` label for label (5,684/5,684 on the public
  scenes under master and on three generated sets under C). This is rule G ("drop ... when all else
  fails") and R1 made measurable: a drop with free ground in reach is a drop before all else failed.
- **Leader on leader per 100 shown labels** (Tom's worst crossing) and **Tom-weighted crossing cost
  per shown label** (his Q05 weights, `judges/weights.js`).
- **Time per layout**: median and maximum over the set's views, ms.
- **Churn rate**: churn / labels compared between consecutive views (bench `stability()`).
- **R11 zoom-in loss rate** (new aggregate): rows lost on zoom-in steps / rows shown at the step
  before; and rows regained.
- **R14 at close zoom**: of the pipe labels still level at 4x, those with room to lie along their
  pipe with the same rows (bench `alignedRoom()`).
- **Leader length** p90 (label heights) and **R7** (pipe labels on their own pipe), reported.

### 1.5 Hypotheses (stated before scoring)

- **H1 (Net3 hides breaks).** At least one placer with zero breaks on E0 has breaks on E1.
- **H2 (the ranking travels).** On E1, C and D show more labels and more values than master, A and
  B, as they do on E0.
- **H3 (time).** Every placer's layout time grows with the number of labels requested; on E1 at
  28 px with n = 5000 at least one placer's median exceeds 1 s. Master's time grows with the whole
  network at a fixed view (E4).
- **H4 (density and shape).** G-missed-room rate rises with density (28 > 44 > 70 px) for every
  placer; by family, downtown is worst and tree best.
- **H5 (churn).** C and D churn more than master on E1, as on the Novato sets.
- **H6 (ID length).** Long IDs lower labels shown % for every placer against short IDs (E2).
- **H7 (the generator is not a toy).** The placers' order on labels shown % and on G-missed-room in
  E3 (published networks) matches their order in E1 (Kendall tau >= 0.6).

### 1.6 Pilot, disclosed

Before this protocol was written, one generated set was run to size the design: suburban, n=1000,
24 px, seed 1 (IDs 'epanet'). On it, A, B, C and D showed 41%, 36%, 56% and 55% of labels; **D had
3 N1 breaks**; median layout times were 0.5, 0.6, 0.4 and 1.9 s. Master's extraction took 140 s
(most of it `shedCrossingSurvivors`), and at 2,500 nodes 655 s. H1 and H3 were therefore written
knowing one instance each; the pilot set is not in the design (24 px is not a design density).

### 1.7 Analysis

For each pair of placers and each outcome, the per-set paired difference over the sets both ran:
**mean difference with a 95% bootstrap interval** (10,000 resamples of sets, seeded) and an **exact
two-sided sign test**, with **Holm's correction** across the ten placer pairs within each outcome.
Master's pairs use the sets master ran (E1 at n <= 1000, E2, E3). Factor effects (family, density,
size, ID style) are reported as per-cell means with bootstrap intervals; H4 and H6 are tested by
paired sign tests on the same networks (density and ID style are paired by construction). H7 by
Kendall's tau between the placers' mean ranks in E1 and E3. A hypothesis is supported when the
test is in the stated direction at Holm-adjusted p < 0.05 for every placer or pair it names.

Rule verdicts (Part 2) follow from the outcomes: a rule is **supported** when the placers that keep
it do better on the outcome it names, **contradicted** when keeping it costs what it claims to
save, and **underdetermined** when the data cannot tell (a rule no placer breaks, or an outcome the
bench cannot see).

### 2.2 Deviations from the protocol

1. **Extraction ran out of room twice.** Three 5000-node pages at once exhausted the 12 GB machine
   (every one hit the 45-minute limit), and the work folder, on the 6 GB `/tmp` RAM disk, filled it.
   The 5000-node networks were then extracted one at a time and the work folder moved to disk.
   Three scene files written while the disk was full were unreadable; they were deleted and
   extracted again from their specs. Scenes are deterministic, so the data are unaffected.
2. **Scoring overlapped extraction.** Cells were scored one at a time while extraction ran (two
   other processes busy), then two at a time; other sessions' harnesses also ran on the machine at
   times. Times are therefore noisier than planned. The seeded random cell order still spreads the
   load evenly over placers, so comparisons between placers stand; absolute times do not.
3. **E4 ran with nothing else of ours running**, but other sessions' processes were live.

No hypothesis, metric, threshold or test was changed.

### 2.4 The hypotheses

- **H1 (Net3 hides breaks): supported.** D, clean on every public scene, breaks N1 in 21 of 216
  generated sets (51 labels over 28 views). Every break is in the two families with **bent pipes**
  (suburban 19/54, downtown 2/54; grid and tree, which have none, 0/108), and every sampled break
  (40 of 40; up to three are kept per view) is a pipe label on another pipe label, or on a node,
  where at least one of the pipes named has a vertex. **The public bench has no bent pipe at all** (Net1, Net2, Net3 and Novato: 0 of 119 links
  with a vertex), so no amount of tuning on it could have found this. A, B and C never break.
  Master breaks in 137 of 144 generated sets, overwhelmingly on **valve symbols** (1,065 of the 1,355
  sampled breaks are a label on a valve or pump symbol; Net3 has pumps but no valves), and with
  leaders through nodes (N3) at the dense views.
- **H2 (the ranking travels): supported for labels, contradicted for values against master.** C
  and D show 26-28 points more labels than master (144/144 sets each) and 5-8 more than A and B
  (Holm p < 0.001). But master shows as many values as C (difference -0.5 points, CI [-1.8, 0.8])
  and more than D (+3.9, CI [2.2, 5.7], Holm p = 0.09): master shows few labels, but shows them
  whole. C shows more values than D in 199 of 216 sets.
- **H3 (time): supported.** Time grows with labels requested for every placer (Spearman 0.14 to
  0.77). At n = 5000 and 28 px the median set's median layout is 511 ms (A), 328 (B), 884 (C) and
  1,075 (D); the worst single views are 10.2 s (A), 1.2 s (B), 4.0 s (C) and 8.1 s (D). **E4: master's
  time grows with the whole network, not the view.** At 4x, with about 33 labels on screen: 201 ms
  at 150 nodes, 678 at 500, 2,051 at 1,000, 6,593 at 2,000 (and at 1x, 52 s at 2,000 nodes). The
  four builders also grow with n at a fixed view, far less: at 70 px and 4x (about 26 labels), C
  7 -> 22 -> 52 ms and D 13 -> 45 -> 171 ms for n = 150, 1000, 5000.
- **H4 (density): mostly supported.** Denser views miss more room for master, C and D in every
  comparison (C: 68 of 72 networks, 28 vs 44 px; D 70/72). Not for A (at 28 px it misses LESS room
  than at 44 px: 55 of 72 networks) and not significantly for B at 28 vs 44 px. The reading: at
  28 px there is often no room within three rows for anyone, so the measure falls for a placer
  that was already dropping too much. By family, H4's order is wrong: the misses are highest on
  grid and tree, lowest on suburban, for every placer. Regular geometry leaves regular gaps that
  a fixed-offset placement does not reach.
- **H5 (churn): mixed.** D churns more than master (4.7 points, Holm p < 0.001); C does not differ
  from master (p = 0.56). B's churn rises from 1% on E0 to 16% on E1 (42% on suburban): its
  per-zoom cache, tuned at Novato's scale, reshuffles on large irregular networks. A holds almost
  perfectly still (0.0% on E1) and shows the fewest values.
- **H6 (ID length): supported for A-D, contradicted for master.** On the same 12 networks, long IDs
  ('J-10234-A') against short (1, 2, 3...) cost A and B 31-32 points of labels, C 26 and D 25 (12/12
  networks each). **Master shows the same share whatever the IDs** (55.3% short, 55.5% long): its
  dropping does not look at the text's size. With short IDs it hid 898 of 899 dropped labels on one
  network where free ground within three rows would have held them.
- **H7 (the generator is not a toy): supported.** The placers' order on the published networks
  matches the generated ones: Kendall tau 0.60 on labels shown, 0.80 on values shown, 1.00 on G
  missed room.

### 2.7 Threats to validity that remain

- **Two published networks only**, both Mediterranean (Cyprus-derived L-Town; C-Town). No large
  North American grid with a licence we could use; the Kentucky and ASCE sets are non-commercial.
- **The generator is ours.** H7 says its placer order matches the published networks, on two of
  them; realism of pipe bends, valve density and ID styles is our guess.
- **Master is measured on the node DOM stub** (nominal 6.55 px per character, not the real font)
  and not at 5,000 nodes; its times include its content pass.
- **Timing on one laptop under shared load** (2.2, deviation 2): relative, not absolute.
- **The G metric is the judges' plain search**: "no room" means no room a 15-degree, three-row,
  straight-leader search finds; a cleverer rearrangement is not credited.
- **What the metrics cannot see:** legibility to a person. Tom's eye remains the judge of what the
  numbers approximate.
- **Untested at scale:** hand-placed labels (N4), Text objects (N5), customers, panning, zooming out,
  field sets other than Novato's, and settings changes.
- **Placers A and B predate R14 and Part A round 2**; their alignment and some drop behaviour are
  not failures against the rules they were given.
- **All builders are the same kind of AI**, as before.

## Proposed rule changes, for Tom

Proposals only. `dev/label-placement-rules.md` is unchanged; each needs his yes.

1. **R10, add scale: "A layout's cost depends on what is on the screen, not on how big the network
   is."** Evidence: E4, master takes 201 ms at 150 nodes and 6,593 ms at 2,000 nodes for the same
   ~33 labels on screen (52 s at 1x), and 11 minutes at 2,500 nodes in the pilot. The builders grow
   too, but by tens of milliseconds. This is the one failure that would bite first on a real
   utility model, and nothing on Net3 can show it.
2. **R10/R15, give the blank a bound: "After a zoom settles, labels are back within about half a
   second on a network of any size. If the layout is not finished by then, show what is placed
   and keep improving it."** (The half second is a placeholder for his number.) Evidence: worst
   single views of 10.2 s (A), 10.3 s (C), 8.1 s (D) and 40 s (master); B's worst is 1.4 s, so a
   bound near a second is reachable. R15 hides every label for that long.
3. **R11, tighten: "Zooming in never hides a row that was showing."** Evidence: every placer lost
   some rows on zoom-in (0.1% to 3%, 6% for B at scale); A shows it can be held at about zero, and
   there is always at least as much room after a zoom-in around the same labels.
4. **G, define "convenient": "Convenient available space is free ground within about three text
   rows of the label's owner, reached by a straight leader that crosses no other leader."**
   Evidence: this definition ranks the placers identically on generated and published networks
   (tau 1.0), matches his Q04 ("the next valley over"), and is already what the judges test.
5. **R1, add: "A label is dropped for lack of room, never by a count or a rank."** Evidence: master's
   drop rate is identical for 3- and 9-character IDs (55% shown either way); with short IDs it hid
   898 of 899 labels that had room. The rule order in R1 already implies this; the data say it
   needs saying.

**Not rules, but the same evidence, for the bench:**

6. **Put bent pipes, valves and large networks on the public bench, and give the judges fresh
   generated scenes every round.** The public bench has no bent pipe and no valve; both hid N1
   breaks (D, master). A fresh secret seed per round also answers the leak threat (builders cannot
   tune to scenes they never see).

**A question rather than a proposal:** at dense views the builders keep IDs and drop values (A shows
69% of labels but 14% of values at 28 px; master shows 49% of labels with 35% of values). R1 says
that order is right. Is a map of bare IDs what he wants at that zoom, or is that the zoom at which
the labeling threshold should hide labels altogether?
